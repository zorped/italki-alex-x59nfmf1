Model

Describe it in a paragraph

These are the three things you tried to explain in class. Read each one aloud, then say it again in your own words.

Where you live, and what you pay

My wife and I rent a one-room apartment for 300 euros a month. It isn't in the city centre, but it is in a decent area. Three years ago we paid 150 euros for a smaller place. Now the rent is 300 euros, partly because of inflation and partly because the apartment is more comfortable. Compared with Moscow, Kaliningrad is much cheaper.

A problem at home

It is very cold in our apartment. Outside it is about one degree during the day, and below zero at night. The radiator isn't working, so the apartment doesn't get warm. I called the heating company, and I also tried to fix it myself. I drained the water from the radiator, but that didn't help. Tomorrow I will call again and ask a repairman to come. I can't ask our landlord, because she is my wife's mother and she lives in another city.

The job search

I'm not looking for a job urgently, but I keep my profile up to date on job websites. Employers post vacancies there, and candidates apply for them. I found a new vacancy on LinkedIn and applied for the position. I haven't been invited to an interview yet, so I'm waiting to hear back.

Two small correctionsWe find a vacancy on a website, not in it. And in Russian a heating battery is a radiator in English. An English battery stores electricity.

Cost, price, rent, fee

You said high expensive and the price for renting an apartment. English chooses the word by what you pay for.

cost
how much you must spend, in generalThe cost of living in Moscow is high.
price
what you pay to buy one thingThe price of a litre of cola is 60 rupees.
rent
the regular payment for using a home or a roomWe pay 300 euros a month in rent.
fee
a payment for a service, or for the right to do somethingThere is a small fee to enter the museum.

High or expensive?Prices, rent and costs are high or low. A thing is expensive or cheap. Say the rent is very high, not high expensive. To compare, say cheaper than, about the same as, twice as expensive as, or about half the price.

Words that were missing

Words you looked for in class, with what each one means.

radiator
the heater on a wall that warms a room, usually with hot waterThe radiator is cold.
repairman, technician
a person whose job is to fix equipmentA technician is coming tomorrow.
landlord
the person who owns a home that you rentOur landlord lives in another city.
mother-in-law
your husband's or your wife's motherMy mother-in-law owns the apartment.
decent
good enough, of acceptable qualityWe rent a decent apartment.
afford
have enough money to pay for somethingWe can't afford a bigger apartment.
eligible
allowed to do something because you meet the rulesI will be eligible to apply after my presentation.
run aground
(a ship) go onto the bottom of shallow water and get stuckThe ship ran aground in the storm.
firecrackers
small explosives that make a loud bangChildren set off firecrackers before Diwali.
fireworks
explosives made for a show, with light, colour and soundThe fireworks lit up the sky.

Day off, public holiday, leave

You asked how many days off people get at Christmas. These words are close, but not the same.

public holiday
a day when most people do not work, set by the governmentChristmas Day is a public holiday in India.
day off
a day when you do not work, often given to you for a reasonI worked on Saturday, so I have Monday as a day off.
leave
time away from work with your employer's permissionI will take two days of annual leave.
vacation
a longer time away from work for rest or travelWe went on vacation for a week.

Ask it naturallyHow many days off do you get at New Year? · How long do people celebrate it? · It is celebrated for one day, but many people start earlier.
